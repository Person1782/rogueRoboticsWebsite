Who we are

Student-led means we teach ourselves everything.

Rogue Robotics is a VEX V5 team from Fremont, California with no coach and no mentors. Every mechanism, autonomous routine, budget line, and outreach event is planned and executed by the seven students on the team. And we learned it all ourselves.

We started mid-season as middle schoolers during the 2024-25 High Stakes game, learning the parts, electronics, and strategy entirely on our own. That trial-and-error year is exactly why the 2025-26 Pushback season looked so different.

The competition

What VEX V5 actually involves

A new game every season. Two-versus-two matches. Robots built, coded, and driven entirely by students.

Watch this season’s game reveal

01

Design & build

We brainstorm mechanisms, model them in CAD, prototype in metal, and iterate until the intake, scoring, and drivetrain survive a full tournament day.

02

Programming

Autonomous routines run the robot on its own for the first fifteen seconds of every match. The rest is driver control code tuned around how our driver actually plays.

03

Strategy

Each match pairs us with a different alliance partner. We scout, adapt between rounds, and pick the scoring path that fits both robots on the field.

04

The engineering notebook

Every decision gets documented - sketches, test data, failures, revisions. Judges read it, and it is part of what won us the Design Award in Sacramento.

2025-26 Pushback season

From barely qualifying to the State Championship

Season start

Ranked near the bottom

We opened the season barely scraping into elimination rounds. Instead of rebuilding once, we iterated constantly during meetings, and long after them.

Competition

Redwood

Judges Award · Quarter-finals

The first payoff. Months of iteration put us in the quarter-finals, and the judges recognized the process behind the robot.

Competition

Sacramento

Design Award · Semi-finals

We traveled to Sacramento, fought to the semi-finals, and won the Design Award for our engineering notebook, live-judged interview, and overall design process. This earned us our spot at the California State Championship.

Next up

California State Championship

Qualified and building toward it, with the VEX World Championship as the goal beyond.

Outreach

Teaching other students to start their own teams

We ran a showcase at Thornton Middle School on how to form and run a self-guided robotics team, then hosted a summer camp where local kids built, programmed, and documented their own robots.

90+ students have come through our events so far.
